Before you begin

AURA is for credible research proposals that can be reviewed, governed, and reported.

Before submitting, a PI should be able to explain the research problem, why it matters to UAP Studies, what methods or sources will be used, who will do the work, what the work is expected to cost, and what outputs will be produced.

The strongest submissions are concrete. They avoid unsupported speculation, define realistic deliverables, address ethics and data management where relevant, and show how the project would contribute to SUAPS's mission of rigorous interdisciplinary inquiry.

Important: AURA submission is not the same as funding approval. A submission begins a review pathway. Projects may advance, be revised, be deferred, or be declined.
Eligibility and readiness

A PI should be ready to lead a serious research effort.

AURA can support academic, professional, and independent research profiles, but the proposal must be credible and manageable.

Eligibility

Research affiliation or verifiable experience

The PI should be affiliated with a recognized academic, research, or professional institution, or should demonstrate verifiable independent research experience.

Capacity

Ability to lead or partner

The PI should be capable of leading a research team, or should partner with collaborators who provide the needed management, methodological, or institutional capacity.

Compliance

No unresolved project issues

Prior SUAPS or donor-funded projects should not have unresolved compliance, reporting, or administrative problems.

Select your phase

Start where the project actually is.

Project maturity, budget, and PI experience determine the most appropriate AURA entry point. The table below is public guidance, not a substitute for SUAPS review.

AURA phase Best fit Required document Typical length
Phase 0 - Conceptual Design Large, early, non-academic, first-time PI, or underdeveloped projects Concept Outline / Letter of Intent 1-2 pages
Phase 1 - Preliminary Design Projects needing feasibility, technical refinement, budget development, or partnership mapping White Paper / Preliminary Proposal 6-8 pages
Phase 2 - Final Design Mature projects ready for formal review and possible portfolio consideration Full Proposal Up to 15 pages
What to include

A strong proposal answers the practical questions first.

The full proposal format is the most complete version of AURA submission. Earlier phases may use abbreviated versions of these same elements.

01

Project identity

Title, PI name, affiliation, collaborators, contact details, phase, budget level, and institutional support if applicable.

02

Research summary

A concise abstract explaining the research problem, objectives, expected outcomes, and relevance to SUAPS.

03

Project description

Background, methods, data sources, instruments, analytical approach, expected results, deliverables, and timeline.

04

Team and support

Roles, qualifications, partner institutions, letters of collaboration, and advisory or mentorship support where needed.

05

Budget and risk

Itemized costs, budget justification, schedule realism, contingencies, co-funding if any, and project risks.

06

Ethics and dissemination

Data management, privacy, consent, intellectual property, broader impacts, publications, public reporting, and outreach.

Submission portal

Submit through the AURA form.

The submission form is the canonical intake point for proposals entering AURA review.

Prepare your proposal document as a PDF before beginning. Where requested, include any supporting materials such as letters of support, prior work, datasets, or institutional documentation.

Submission cycles, deadlines, and review timing may be updated by SUAPS. Use the current submission form and public instructions as the source of record for the active cycle.
