AURA review process

How proposals are reviewed.

A public explanation of the review pathway, not the internal scoring manual.

AURA reviews proposals in structured phases. Review considers scholarly merit, methodological soundness, feasibility, alignment, impact, and team capacity while preserving the confidentiality needed for serious evaluation.

Review philosophy

The goal is constructive gatekeeping.

AURA exists to help SUAPS identify which proposals are ready for structured support, which need refinement, and which should not advance within the Society's research portfolio. The review process is meant to be serious but not opaque.

The public site should tell researchers what kinds of questions reviewers will ask. It should not publish internal thresholds, detailed weightings, reviewer identities, donor visibility rules, or unresolved governance procedures.

Review boundary: The public criteria below are guidance for applicants. SUAPS may use additional internal records, expert consultation, or governance review when making formal decisions.
Review pathway

A proposal moves through a structured sequence.

The exact path depends on phase, completeness, project maturity, and available reviewer expertise.

01

Receipt and administrative check

SUAPS confirms that the submission is complete enough to evaluate and that the materials match the requested AURA phase.

02

Phase classification

The project is considered as a Conceptual Design, Preliminary Design, or Final Design submission based on maturity, budget, and PI experience.

03

Substantive review

Reviewers examine the intellectual case, method, feasibility, team, budget, ethics, data plan, expected outputs, and relevance to SUAPS priorities.

04

Decision and feedback

SUAPS issues a decision outcome and, where appropriate, provides guidance for revision, advancement, deferral, or closure.

05

Portfolio or project handoff

Projects that advance may move toward donor portfolio consideration, project formation, reporting setup, or later-phase execution controls.

What reviewers consider

AURA looks for rigor, feasibility, and fit.

The public review criteria should be clear enough to help PIs improve their proposals without exposing internal scoring mechanics.

Merit

Scientific or scholarly merit

Does the proposal ask a meaningful question, build on relevant work, and contribute to UAP Studies or related fields?

Method

Methodological soundness

Are the methods, sources, instruments, analytical procedures, or interpretive framework appropriate to the question?

Feasibility

Budget, timeline, and execution

Can the proposed work realistically be completed with the team, schedule, resources, and budget described?

Impact

Contribution and broader value

Would the project strengthen research practice, public understanding, education, collaboration, or interdisciplinary knowledge?

Team

Capacity and governance

Does the team have the experience, partners, roles, and institutional support needed to carry out the project responsibly?

Care

Ethics, data, and reporting

Does the proposal address privacy, consent, data management, intellectual property, publication, and public reporting where relevant?

Possible outcomes

Not every strong idea is ready at the same time.

AURA review should help sort proposals into the right next step.

Advance

Ready for the next step

The proposal may advance to the next AURA phase, project formation, or further portfolio consideration.

Revise

Promising but incomplete

The PI may be asked to revise and resubmit with stronger methods, budget, team, evidence, or scope.

Defer

Potentially relevant, not ready now

The project may be deferred pending timing, clarification, reviewer availability, or alignment with future cycles.

Decline

Not appropriate for AURA

The proposal may be declined if it lacks fit, rigor, feasibility, or sufficient basis for responsible review.

What remains internal

Some governance details should not be published here.

The public review-process page intentionally does not disclose internal score sheets, scoring weights, minimum thresholds, reviewer assignments, donor access rules, or confidential proposal handling procedures.

That boundary protects researchers, reviewers, donors, and SUAPS itself. The public page explains the review logic; internal materials preserve the detailed governance mechanics.
